Architectural Design

Net-zero green building

Design a zero energy building, holistically addressing the carbon impact of it by providing a defensible rationale for selection of a retrofit or new construction project to meet the goals stated by the Solar Decathlon Design Challenge. As well as to quantify the carbon impact of the design and achieve decarbonization goals.

The division choosen for the design is residential, affordable housing building and the main aspects to focus from the ten aspects that the Solar Decathlon Deisgn Challenge establishes are Architecture, Market, Community and Presentation.

The Verdant Ascent project presents a comprehensive solution to contemporary housing challenges in Taiwan, integrating sustainability, community, affordability, and engineering innovation into a replicable model for net-zero multifamily residential development. Situated in Xinwu District, Taoyuan, the project responds to the area's smart city aspirations and demographic transitions by prioritizing adaptable unit layouts, energy efficiency, and environmental stewardship.

The goals and objectives are as follows:

Goals:

  1. Architectural Design Optimization:
    • Investigate and develop efficient architectural designs that maximize natural light, ventilation, passive solar heating and cooling.
    • Explore innovative materials and construction techniques that enhance sustainability and reduce costs.
  2. Community Engagement and Needs Assessment:
    • Conduct surveys and focus groups to understand the specific needs and preferences of the target community.
    • Develop strategies to foster community engagement and participation in the design process.
  3. Market Analysis and Financial Viability:
    • Conduct a thorough market analysis to identify demand for affordable housing in the area.
    • Evaluate financial models and incentives (e.g., tax credits, subsidies) that can make the project economically viable.
  4. Effective Presentation and Communication:
    • Develop a clear and compelling narrative about the project's benefits, focusing on sustainability, affordability, and community impact.
    • Create engaging visual materials (e.g., renderings, videos) to effectively communicate the project's design and value proposition to stakeholders.

Objectives:

  1. Design and Sustainability: Design a multifamily affordable housing project that achieves net-zero energy status while maintaining affordability. By using energy modeling tools to optimize building orientation, insulation, and renewable energy integration.
  2. Community Integration: Ensure the project aligns with community needs and preferences. By conducting community surveys and workshops to gather feedback and incorporate it into the design process.
  3. Market Feasibility: Determine the financial feasibility of the project and identify potential funding sources. By analyzing local market trends, assess government incentives, and evaluate partnerships with private investors.
  4. Presentation and Communication: Develop a persuasive presentation that highlights the project’s unique features and benefits. By creating detailed visual aids, prepare a comprehensive project report, and rehearse presentations for clarity and impact.

Timber Structure Design: 12 meter pedestrian bridge for Elementary School Pond at Xitou Nature Education Park

The south side primary school pond currently only has stepping stones that are very slippery, so if you want to go around the school pond, you have to take a detour and go up and down the stairs. Therefore, Fu Shou Industrial, a well-known food and feed company in central Taiwan, decided to donate to the National Taiwan University Experimental Forest to build a pedestrian bridge. The Experimental Forest contacted Professor Hervé Capart （卡艾瑋） and Professor Masatoshi Tomita (富田匡俊) of the Civil Engineering Department at National Taiwan University, with the idea that together with students from the department, the pedestrian bridge can become a reality.

The design requirements are as follows: Pedestrian bridge size 12 meters length, with a witdh of 2 meters. The structural materials to use are wood (Taiwanese domestic), or a steel-wood hybrid system design, the live load to be considered of span 0~8m (including 8m) 400kgf/m^2. The elements to be design include the Main structure, foundation structure, bridge deck, railings, cost, others (lighting, signs, etc.)

Unconventional Sitting object - The Escape Seat / 避難座位​

The task was to propose and build a wooden design of an object where one can sit on, though it has to be unconventional and needs to convey a feeling, sensation or emotion. An specific location for the object needs also to be selected and the object has to interact with the space either by complementing, disrupting, fusioning with it and so on. A budget was provided to buy the necessary materials and extra tools if needed, we managed to stay below budget, it was also needed to build a scale model of the design and an ortographic projection of it.

Together with my team, we choose to combine anxiety with peacefulness, two opposite yet coexisting emotions. I got inspired by the Emergency Escape Sling system found all around Taiwan and proposed to modify to sit at the edge of the building instead of its original purpose that is to descend from a building in case of emergency. After several design proposals and for safety reasons we finally came up with a suitable concept. The sitting device evokes anxiety to the user during the assembly process, but once seated, the space conveys peace since the framed metal rectangle directs the eyesight of the user to the view of the city.

The seat mimics the original escape sling made out of metal, but when building with wood it sparks curiosity to the user and forces him/her to pay attention to the device that usually goes unnotice. In a similar manner that the emotions are opposite, the metal and steel of the space contrast with the wood of the object, the first 2 heightens anxiety, meanwhile the wood promotes relaxation through tactile experience.

Additionally we challenge ourselves to create a design that uses no additional elements when converting from the standing position to the seat position, to achieve it, each part had to serve more than a single purpose, for instance, the base plate serves as the seating pad. We also focused on making the structure as lightweight as possible and of easy assembly, so that one person can assemble it.

Some other components that compliments to the design are the red ratchet strap, being the single element with a vibrant color it denotes the sense of anxiety and danger. Small details such as; the design of the poster that contains the instructions on how to assemble the device and the box were components like the ratchet strap can be stored inside when the device is in it standing position, are those details that makes the concept much more realistic.

Random Password Generator (RPG)

By using Python in the web-based environment JupyterLab Notebook, tool offered in the Graphical User Interface (GUI) Anaconda Navigator, a progam was created to generate random passwords according to the parameters set by the user.

If they user would like for the program to use only a limited range of letters, number and special characters, he/she needs to add those parameters and later the code will ask the user how many paswwords would like to create, the numbers of characters for the password(s) to have and if it wants to use special characters or not. Since, some websites don't allow the use of special characters in the passwords and they usually set a minimum amount of "strings".

The program will then display the passwords wich all contains a random arrangement of number, upper and lowercase letters and special characters if the user specifies to have them.

Falling in Love - Two-track marble ball roller coaster

The objective of the project was to propose and build a design of a Two-track roller coaster, one of the tracks had to be exciting, fun, fast and with turns and loops, while the second track had to slow and less extreme. However both marble balls had to arrive at then of the the roller coaster at the same time.

It was necessary to program and build with LEGO a mechanism that identifies which ball is coming, and depending of it it had to swtich lanes. We decided to program the arduino with a light sensor, since one ball was transparent and the other was made out of metal, so when the ball passes through the tunnel at the beginning of the track, part of the track where the light was installed at the ground floor of the tunnel and the light sensor at the top "ceiling" of the tunnel beind directed to the LED, if the ball was the transparent one the light will still go through so but the intensity will be reduced, so then switching mechanism made with LEGO redirects the ball to the slow-pace track. Meanwhile, when the metal ball goes through the tunnel for a brief moment the sensor will stop receiving light, so the ball is redirected to the fast-pace track.

As a bonus it was suggested to established a them for the rollercoaster, since in real life, in any amusement parks they have a theme, so we decided to name it "Falling in Love" since the balls are quite literally "falling" and as to follow the dynamic we include heartshapes around the track and for the balls to end up at heart railing installed at the bottom of the roller coaster.

Together with my team we were awarded as the popular track and also as the most reliable track, since we set a theme for the roller coasater and we not only managed for the balls to arrive at the same time at the end of the roller coaster but also several times the track where tested and we excelled at consistency and accuracy.
