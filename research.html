Research

Sustainable Construction and Smart Cities

Comparative Analysis of Heat-Reflective Tile and Paint Materials for Urban Heat Island (UHI) Mitigation and Outdoor Thermal Comfort in Urban Taipei

Abstract: In recent years, applying cool materials to roofs and pavements has become a popular strategy to mitigate the Urban Heat Island (UHI) effect. However, less attention has been given to their use on vertical surfaces like walls and facades. Highly reflective (HR) materials effectively reduce surface temperatures and building heat absorption, helping lower cooling energy use. Retroreflective (RR) materials differ in that they reflect solar radiation back to its source, potentially offering greater improvements to the outdoor thermal environment. This study used ENVI-met software to simulate a representative area of Taipei. The baseline model featured concrete buildings, while two alternative cases applied high-albedo tiles or paint to the horizontal and vertical surfaces of eight buildings. Both materials reduced air temperature compared to concrete, with maximum differences at 14:00 of 0.78 °C (tile) and 0.64 °C (paint). However, they also increased mean radiant temperature (MRT) and Physiological Equivalent Temperature (PET). The tile outperformed the paint, increasing MRT and PET by 9.8 °C and 3.14 °C, respectively, compared to increases of 11.43 °C and 11.51 °C with the paint.

Hydrology

Before and after - Evaluation of Urbanization effect on Adama City, Ethiopia

Abstract: In recent years, there has been a surge in studying how urbanization affects flooding due to severe weather events worldwide. The research seeks to analyze and compare the effect of urbanization in Adama, Ethiopia, in 1997 (before urbanization) and 2023 (after urbanization), mainly by the runoff, considering the largest rainfall experienced in those 2 years, by first categorizing the hydrologic soil groups that the area posses, and its land use. This information is obtained by the usage of a geographic information system (GIS), Google Earth Pro, in combination with the Soil Conservation Service (1972) method for abstractions. It was found that urbanization in Adama City has significantly impacted runoff intensity, with a direct relationship between impervious surfaces and runoff. Under normal moisture conditions, runoff increased by up to 2.4% with a total rainfall of 19.685 inches. However, during wet conditions, which are common during the rainy season, runoff potential increases due to saturated soil, making the area more prone to flooding. Despite this, the city's soil has good infiltration capabilities, which helps mitigate some of the runoff effects. The study emphasizes the importance of implementing sustainable urban drainage systems that mimic natural patterns to reduce runoff and minimize disruptions to hydrological processes.
